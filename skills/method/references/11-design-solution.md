# 11. Design for the job, step by step

## What it gives you

Design that starts from what users are trying to achieve, not from a feature list, stays purposeful. Each decision can be checked against the outcomes it should improve, which leads to sharper, better targeted solutions.

## Steps

1. Bring the job-based journey map into your design tool.
2. For each job, note its main desired outcomes.
3. For each job, explore possible solutions and sketch an interface.
4. Test each sketch: does it help get this job done and reach its main outcomes?
5. Iterate until each job has its most relevant solution.
6. Zoom out and check the whole journey for consistency.
7. Refine up to a high-fidelity mockup.

## Running it with Claude

Claude turns the journey (main job, ordered steps, outcomes) into a design brief, including the alternatives to beat and the Anxiety and Habit forces to overcome, and reviews a concept against the outcomes of the job it serves.
