# 5. Build the interview guide from jobs and outcomes

## What it gives you

A guide organized around jobs and outcomes keeps the interview on what people are trying to accomplish and how they judge success. You learn more about motivations, friction and the decisions they make, and the feedback is easier to turn into action.

## Steps

1. Choose the jobs and desired outcomes that best represent your users' main priorities.
2. Open with broad questions about the main jobs.
3. Narrow down: questions on each job step, then on sub-jobs and their outcomes.
4. Check that the guide covers every selected main job and outcome.
5. Leave room for discovery with open questions such as "What are you trying to achieve with this?" and "What does success look like for you?"

Navigation tip: asking "Why?" moves the conversation up toward the main job; asking "How?" moves it down toward steps and sub-jobs.

## Running it with Claude

Claude assembles the guide straight from the base, one section per main job, with probes for each step and outcome. It marks the jobs that are still hypotheses so the interview spends time where evidence is missing, and adds questions on alternatives and on the forces of progress.
