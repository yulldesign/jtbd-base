# 3. Turn jobs and outcomes into hypotheses

## What it gives you

Hypotheses rooted in what users are trying to get done keep innovation meaningful and effort focused on changes that genuinely improve their experience. Since every hypothesis points to a job or an outcome, it stays inside the team's scope and can be ranked with the same priorities as the jobs and outcomes themselves.

## Steps

1. Gather the whole team (meeting or workshop).
2. Restate the scope: performers, jobs, outcomes and the journey map.
3. Discuss hypotheses that start from a job, an outcome, or both.
4. Review and sharpen them together.
5. Rank them on several criteria, one of which is the priority of the jobs and outcomes they target.

## Running it with Claude

Claude drafts each hypothesis as "We believe [performer] struggles to [outcome] during [job step]. We will know we are right when [signal]." and ranks the list by the opportunity score of the linked outcomes, or by strength of evidence when no scores exist yet.
